## 8. Ethics and decision-making

### 8.1 Case-by-case rule-making [Moderate; Strong in the Vinaya]
*Evidence: 114 texts in 18 collections, 16 of them early; 128 lines support, 7 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 12, early Khuddaka 4, Jātaka 12, late Khuddaka 5, Vinaya 65, Abhidhamma 1, Dhammapada stories 15.*

- **Rules follow incidents.** He refuses to lay down a code in advance (Bu-Vb-Pj1:3.4.5–3.4.6; MN 65:30.2–30.3). The pattern: an incident, a complaint, "Is it true, monks?", a rebuke, a rule (Bu-Vb-Sk37:1.2–1.16).
- **Allowances and revision.** "Monks, I allow you to end the robe season", to satisfy a donor (Bi-Vb-Pc30:1.1.2–1.1.8). Sick monks get an exception (Bu-Vb-Sk37:2.1–2.10).
- **Exemptions built in.** No offence if unintentional, unknowing, insane, or a first offender (Bu-Vb-Sk37:2.13–2.24). The perception matrices (Bi-Vb-Pc95:2.2.1–2.2.6) are probably the work of later jurists.
- **Due process.** A motion, three announcements, silence as consent (Kd 13:3). A penalty needs the accused's presence, questioning, and admission (Kd 14:12.3.2.1; Kd 11:2.1.1–2.1.2); a unanimous Saṅgha that skips this "is at fault" (Kd 9:6.3.2). An Abhidhamma text even counts needless remorse as a defilement (Pp 2.5:L5).
- **Complications.** Monks feel harried: "We’re well rid of that Great Ascetic" (DN 16:6.20.3–6.20.4, an old monk). Rulings follow a rule's technical scope, not the harm: a monk who handled a sleeping novice's genitals gets only "an offense of wrong conduct" under the suspension rule (Bu-Vb-Ss1:5.9.10–5.9.11).

### 8.2 Intention is the key to kamma [Strong]
*Evidence: 196 texts in 25 collections, 52 of them early; 208 lines support, 27 complicate, 2 run against. By group: Nikāyas 45, early Khuddaka 7, Jātaka 17, late Khuddaka 20, Vinaya 61, Abhidhamma 29, Dhammapada stories 17.*

- "It is intention that I call deeds" (AN 6.63:33.3); "Mind precedes all things" (Book 11's rendering of Dhp 1–2, passage 1); mental deeds are "the most blameworthy" (MN 56:4.11). His act of truth for a woman in labour rests on not having "intentionally taken the life of a living creature" (MN 86:15.2–15.8). Book 10 found that "an act is judged by its intention" survives any audit.
- **Limit: views.** Denying the afterlife is itself wrong conduct of mind (MN 41:10.5); wrong view leads to hell (DN 12:10.15).
- **Limit: the law and the later layers.** Some Vinaya offences stand even when the monk thought the act allowable (Bu-Vb-NP1:4.3); "There are offenses that are committed unintentionally" (Pvr 18:1.1); outcome grades the same intent (Pvr 1.2:3.3); a nun's prudent motive does not excuse her (Bi-Vb-Ss3:3.4). Nāgasena holds that unknowing evil brings greater demerit (Mil 3.7.8:1.3). Intention governs kamma in the discourses, not all liability in the law.

### 8.3 Flexibility [Moderate; Strong in the Vinaya]
*Evidence: 140 texts in 17 collections, 24 of them early; 159 lines support, 9 complicate, 1 runs against. By group: Nikāyas 21, early Khuddaka 3, Jātaka 13, late Khuddaka 12, Vinaya 83, Dhammapada stories 8.*

- "If it wishes, after my passing the Saṅgha may abolish the lesser and minor training rules" (DN 16:6.3.1). The first council could not agree which rules were minor, and so kept them all (Kd 21:1.9.3–1.9.35; Book 10, analysis 2).
- He waives the probation for converts: "I have recognized individual differences in this matter" (DN 8:24.1–24.2). He refuses to make austerity compulsory (Kd 17:3.15.10–3.15.16).
- Even the teachings are to be let go once crossed (MN 22:14.1). The freed person has "banished both merit and evil" (Dhp 256–272) and "escaped the chain of both good and bad deeds" (Dhp 383–423; Dhp 33–43): ethics is the means, not the goal.
- **Limits.** He will not ordain Pukkusāti without bowl and robes, and Pukkusāti is killed by a cow while looking for them (MN 140:34.4–35.2). Money is never allowed (SN 42.10:3.6–3.7). He endorses Upasena: "One should not lay down new rules, nor should one get rid of the existing ones" (Bu-Vb-NP15:1.2.11–1.2.12).

### 8.4 Accommodation to society [Moderate; Strong in the Vinaya; hard case]
*Evidence: 178 texts in 18 collections, 34 of them early; 203 lines support, 9 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 29, early Khuddaka 5, Jātaka 13, late Khuddaka 3, Vinaya 117, Abhidhamma 1, Dhammapada stories 10.*

The same pattern appears four times in one chapter. He admits people the society rejects, then bars the category when powerful people complain:

- soldiers in the king's service (Kd 1:40.4.9)
- notorious criminals, after people fled from the ordained Aṅgulimāla (Kd 1:41.1.7)
- escaped criminals (Kd 1:42.2.13)
- slaves, after people called the monastics "untouchable" (Kd 1:47.1.8–47.1.11)

Women candidates are screened for slavery, debt, and the consent of parents and husband (Kd 20:17.1.9–17.1.10), and a married girl may be ordained at twelve (Bi-Pm:134.1). The Vinaya's recurring reason, "This will affect people’s confidence", explains it (Bu-Vb-Ss1:1.2.18–1.2.19); digging is banned because "People regard the earth as conscious" (Bu-Vb-Pc10:1.13–1.14). The discourses keep shrine offerings (DN 16:1.4.20), and told of a mass imprisonment he says nothing against the king (SN 3.10:4.1–4.2).

**Favourable reading:** prudence that let the community survive; and in the Second Council story the money rule outranks donors' goodwill (Kd 22:1.2.2–1.2.3). **Cost:** some of those most in need of refuge were turned away.

### 8.5 Correcting course after harm [Thin; striking]
*Evidence: 32 texts in 9 collections, 3 of them early; 40 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 3, Jātaka 7, late Khuddaka 1, Vinaya 18, Abhidhamma 1, Dhammapada stories 2.*

He praises meditation on the body's unattractiveness and goes into retreat for half a month. Monks become disgusted with their bodies and kill themselves and one another. On his return he asks: "Ānanda, why is the Sangha of monks so reduced?" He then teaches mindfulness of breathing as "peaceful and sublime" (Bu-Vb-Pj3:1.1.2–1.3.1). The early parallel tells the same story (SN 54.9:3.3–6.3). Neither records remorse. Book 5 noted the harm this practice can do without balance.

- He takes back monks he had dismissed once he thinks of the juniors among them (SN 22.80:2.2–2.3; MN 67:10.1).
- Rules are amended after they harm: nuns made to travel apart "were robbed and raped by bandits" (Bu-Vb-Pc27:2.8; Bu-Vb-Pc28:2.9); women candidates are humiliated by monks' questions (Kd 20:17.2.4).

### 8.6 His working decision tests [Moderate]
*Evidence: 80 texts in 14 collections, 28 of them early; 91 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 27, early Khuddaka 1, Jātaka 11, late Khuddaka 2, Vinaya 30, Dhammapada stories 9.*

- **Harm** to oneself, others, or both, checked before, during, and after an act (MN 61:9.2–10.2).
- **Reciprocity** (SN 55.7:4.6–4.7); a Jātaka verse narrows it to tit-for-tat (Ja 223:L20).
- **Skilful qualities** grow or decline (DN 21:2.4.10–2.4.13); even his teaching is known by whether it leads to peace and awakening (AN 7.83:2.3–2.5).
- **What the wise praise** (AN 3.65:26.3; MN 54:6.4).
- **No bias** from "favoritism, hostility, stupidity, and cowardice" (AN 4.17:1.3; Kd 16:11.2.5).
- **Self, world, and teaching** as the three things one can "put in charge" of one's conduct (AN 3.40:1.1–1.14).

### 8.7 Foresight of decline [Moderate]
*Evidence: about 30 texts in about 9 collections, 18 of them early; about 36 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 18, Vinaya 7, late Khuddaka 5.*

- "The true teaching doesn’t disappear like a ship that sinks all at once" (SN 16.13:3.3); people, not the elements, destroy it (SN 16.13:3.1–3.2). Monks grown soft will give Māra his chance (SN 20.8:3.3–3.5).
- Unless learned monks pass them on, "the discourses are cut off at the root" (AN 4.160:6.1–6.2). Past Buddhas' teachings lasted where they "laid down training rules" (Bu-Vb-Pj1:3.3.3–3.3.4), and rules are laid down for "the longevity of the true Teaching" (Bu-Vb-NP15:1.3.8).
- He claims no exemption: "I will be fully extinguished" (SN 15.20:5.8). The late Buddhavaṃsa ends its chapters on past Buddhas with the vanishing of their power and fame (Bv 3:30.1–30.4).
- **Hard case.** "The true teaching will remain only five hundred years", because women were ordained (AN 8.51:26.2–26.3; Kd 20:1.6.5–1.6.6; section 9.2).
