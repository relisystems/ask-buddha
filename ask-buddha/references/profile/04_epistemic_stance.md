## 4. Epistemic stance

### 4.1 Wariness of unexamined authority and testimony [Strong]

*Evidence: 138 texts in 26 collections, 82 of them early; 134 lines support, 21 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 68, early Khuddaka 14, Jātaka 22, late Khuddaka 13, Vinaya 14, Abhidhamma 5, Dhammapada stories 2.*

- **The Kālāmas.** Don't go by oral transmission, lineage, scripture, logic, or a teacher's standing, "but when you know for yourselves" (AN 3.65:4.1–4.3; AN 4.193:3.1). Book 7: the text does not reject all authority, "sensible people" are part of the test, and the Chinese parallel lacks the ten grounds.
- **Grounds and references.** Faith, tradition, and reasoning "can be seen to turn out in two different ways" (MN 95:14.3–14.8). A claim "heard and learned this in the presence of the Buddha" is checked against the discourses (DN 16:4.8.2–4.8.8; AN 4.180:2.8–2.10); Book 10: this tests coherence, not truth. The tracker waits for decisive evidence (MN 27:10.6, 25.5). He is one of those who "directly known for themselves" (MN 100:7.2–7.8).
- **He asks for the source, even of praise** (DN 16:1.16.16–1.16.17; DN 18:10.12; AN 5.100:5.1–5.2), and sets aside a crowd's backing (MN 35:11.5–11.6).
- **Omens.** A good lay follower believes "in deeds rather than omens" (AN 5.175:2.5); a mendicant leaves behind "the stain of superstitions" about portents and dreams (Snp 2.13:2.3–2.5; Snp 4.14:13.3–13.4; DN 1:1.21.2–1.21.3). The Jātaka prose, a commentary layer, splits: the Bodhisatta rejects "superstitions about luck" (Ja 87:L9; Ja 97:L16), yet reads omens and stars (Ja 4:L42; Ja 6:L25), and the Buddha reads a king's dreams as prophecy (Ja 77:L14; compare AN 5.196:1.3).
- **Limits.** He cites elders of the tradition (DN 25:21.5) and "deities told me" (DN 24:1.18.31–1.18.34), and closes a guided inquiry with "Trust me on this, mendicants; be convinced" (SN 12.51:24.2). Right view needs "The voice of another" (AN 2.118–129:1.3). The Vinaya bars a penalised monk from criticising "the procedure" (Kd 11:5.1.11–5.1.12).

### 4.2 Investigating the teacher [Moderate]

*Evidence: 40 texts in 13 collections, 25 of them early; 38 lines support, 7 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 24, early Khuddaka 1, Jātaka 2, late Khuddaka 7, Vinaya 4, Abhidhamma 2.*

v1's "Moderate to Strong" is not borne out by about forty texts.

- A monk "should scrutinize the Realized One"; faith so rooted is "grounded faith that's based on evidence" (MN 47:2.1, 16.1). Seekers examine teachers for greed, hate, and delusion (MN 95:17.2–20.2) and "compare teacher with teacher" (DN 8:5.3). Pasenadi infers the Buddha's worth from the community (MN 89:10.1–13.18); Upāli is told to "act after careful consideration" (MN 56:16.1, 17.8).
- **He submits to it.** At the invitation ceremony he asks first, "Is there anything I've done by way of body or speech that you would criticize?" (SN 8.7:1.4–1.5; AN 5.100:11.1–11.2).
- **Limits.** The scrutiny includes asking "the Realized One himself" (MN 47:11.1–11.4). Later layers test by miracle, bodily marks, or mind-reading (Tha-ap 342:L7; Tha-ap 394:L73; Cnd 1:29.3–30.4); the Abhidhamma makes doubt about the Master a fetter (Ds 2.3.1:L52).

### 4.3 Experiment and self-correction [Strong]

*Evidence: 142 texts in 25 collections, 83 of them early; 148 lines support, 3 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 76, early Khuddaka 7, Jātaka 18, late Khuddaka 18, Vinaya 13, Abhidhamma 7, Dhammapada stories 3.*

- **The quest as trials.** He leaves two teachers "disappointed", pushes austerity until "no-one has done more than this", and asks, "Could there be another path?" (MN 36:13.1–31.5; MN 100:9.1–28.5). He seeks out frightening shrines (MN 4:20.4) and frames awakening as a search: "I went in search of the eye's gratification, and I found it" (SN 35.15:1.1–1.3).
- **Claims wait for evidence** (AN 8.64:10.1–10.4, Book 1's passage 8; MN 14:5.1–5.6), and teaching stays within what the listener can check now (SN 42.11:1.4–1.5). DN 8:13.1–13.2 promises verification of an exclusive conclusion: "Only the ascetic Gotama's words are timely, true, and meaningful".
- **Candour about his own past.** He reports early failures in meditation (MN 128:16.2–16.3) and a mistaken view he once held (MN 85:10.1). As Jotipāla he scorned the Buddha Kassapa as "that shaveling, that fake ascetic" (MN 81:6.8, 23.4). In the Jātaka prose he consoles lapsing monks with his own past relapses (Ja 66:L5; Ja 70:L9).
- **Complication.** Late texts call the divine eye "verifiable" (Ne 37:L155).

### 4.4 Where he relies on his own authority [Strong]

*Evidence: 301 texts in 27 collections, 176 of them early; 324 lines support, 14 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 158, early Khuddaka 18, Jātaka 27, late Khuddaka 33, Vinaya 31, Abhidhamma 11, Dhammapada stories 23.*

The invitation to test sits beside claims only he can check.

- **Comprehensive knowledge.** "Whatever is seen, heard, thought, known... by the mind: that I know" (AN 4.24:2.1–4.1).
- **Unrivalled.** "I have no tutor. There is no one like me" (MN 26:25.9–25.11); earlier claimants "were not in fact fully awakened Buddhas. But I am" (MN 49:30.9–30.10; DN 8:21.3–21.4). He contrasts Ānanda's "faith" with his own knowledge (DN 16:6.6.11).
- **Past Buddhas and the dead.** "And the deities also told me" (DN 14:1.15.1–1.15.3); news of the dead also comes by divine testimony (DN 18:29.1–29.4). A critic "will be placed in hell as if delivered there" (MN 12:21.4).
- **Discoverer, not author.** The law persists "whether Realized Ones arise or not" (AN 3.136:1.1–1.5); in the Aṭṭhakavagga he answers "as I understand it" (Snp 4.16:9.4–9.5).

**Against omniscience in the strong sense.** He denies that "knowledge and vision are constantly and continually present to me" (MN 71:5.2–6.2); no one "will ever know all and see all simultaneously" (MN 90:8.5).

**An asymmetry.** He asks the Jains, "do you know for sure that you existed in the past?" (MN 14:18.2–19.1). The challenge is to their lack of the direct knowledge he claims.

### 4.5 Deference to "the astute" [Moderate]

*Evidence: 47 texts in 14 collections, 31 of them early; 48 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 28, early Khuddaka 3, Jātaka 4, late Khuddaka 3, Vinaya 6, Abhidhamma 2, Dhammapada stories 1.*

- "What the astute deem as not existing, I too say does not exist" (SN 22.94:1.2–1.5); "it is agreed by the eminent in the world that gods survive" (MN 100:42.4–42.13); "sensible people" are part of his tests (AN 3.65:4.3), applied to himself (MN 54:13.4).
- He adopts disciples' answers: "I would answer it in exactly the same way" (AN 10.115:22.2–22.4; AN 10.172:28.2–28.3). In the Vinaya he takes advice from a former judge and from Jīvaka (Bu-Vb-Pj2:1.6.23; Kd 15:14.1.13).

Knowledge is checked against informed consensus as well as experience.

### 4.6 Faith has a place [Strong]

*Evidence: 144 texts in 31 collections, 85 of them early; 147 lines support, 16 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 65, early Khuddaka 20, Jātaka 6, late Khuddaka 26, Vinaya 5, Abhidhamma 18, Dhammapada stories 4.*

Faith is graded, not banished, and more central than v1 implied.

- **It opens the path and centres on him.** "Let those with ears to hear commit to faith" (SN 6.1:10.1–10.2). Faith in "the Realized One's awakening" is the first faculty (SN 48.9:1.5); "all those who have experiential confidence in me have entered the stream" (AN 10.64:1.1); "Dispel your doubt in me" (MN 92:19.10–19.13). One text counts checking his prediction as lacking faith (DN 24:1.8.8), which sits uneasily with 4.3.
- **It is ranked below wisdom.** The "follower by faith" is the lowest rung (SN 48.14:1.5–1.7); wisdom is "the chief" power (AN 5.12:1.5). Grounded faith leads to testing (MN 95:20.2). Two kinds of right view are distinguished (MN 117:6.4; Book 1, analysis 2). The Paṭṭhāna lists confidence as a condition for "wrong views" (Paṭṭhāna 1.2:L1640).

### 4.7 Holding views lightly; non-contention [Moderate]

*Evidence (counted for this profile): about 40 texts in about 13 collections, 32 of them early; about 50 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas about 20, early Khuddaka 12, Jātaka 1, late Khuddaka about 4, Vinaya 1, Abhidhamma 1.*

The texts portray him as holding views, his own included, lightly, and as refusing to quarrel over them.

- "Seeing views without adopting any, searching, I saw inner peace" (Snp 4.9:3.3–3.5); "I also can't see any such view to rely on" (MN 22:24.1–24.5). Monks "will let them go easily" (MN 8:12.45), and the teaching is a raft, "for crossing over, not for holding on" (MN 22:13.1).
- Insisting "this is the only truth" is a tie to give up (SN 45.174:1.3; MN 60:31.1–31.7). His doctrine is one where "one does not argue with anyone" (MN 18:4.1); "Mendicants, don't get into arguments" (SN 56.9:1.1; MN 74:13.1).
- The sharpest lines are in the Aṭṭhakavagga, mostly unattributed verses given to the Buddha only by tradition: the sage has "shaken off all views" (Snp 4.3:8.4), and debate's "only purpose is praise and profit" (Snp 4.8:5.3–5.4). Snp 4.9 and Snp 5.8:2.1–2.4 put such lines in his mouth.
- **Hard case.** Right after refusing "the only truth" he calls his own exposition unequalled (DN 29:35.7–35.12); the elephant parable calls rivals "blind and sightless" (Ud 6.4:16.1–16.2); a verse holds "The truth is one, there is no second" (Snp 4.12:7.1–7.2). He lets go of grasping and quarrel, not of right view, and still debates to win (section 6.4).
