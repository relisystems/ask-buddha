## 9. Views on society

### 9.1 Caste and birth [Strong; mixed]

*Evidence: 153 texts in 26 collections, 65 of them early; 138 lines support, 50 complicate, 16 run against. By group: Nikāyas 44, early Khuddaka 21, Jātaka 38, late Khuddaka 27, Vinaya 11, Abhidhamma 2, Dhammapada stories 10.*

**What the texts do:**

- They attack the brahmins' inborn superiority on biological grounds (DN 27:4.6; MN 93:5.9) and argue that classes are local and mobile (MN 93:6.5).
- "the differences between humans are not defined by birth", as species are (MN 98:9.1–9.4); names are convention (Snp 3.9:60.1–64.4).
- The brahmin is redefined by conduct (Dhp 393–395): "Don’t ask about birth, ask about conduct" (SN 7.9:3.1).
- Liberation is the same in every class (MN 90:12.6), and classes lose their names on going forth, like rivers in the sea (AN 8.19:14.2).

**Where they cut the other way:**

- **Inside the frame.** Against Ambaṭṭha he ranks aristocrats above brahmins (DN 3:1.26.1) and uses the youth's descent from a slave-girl as a weapon (DN 3:1.15.6).
- **Kamma re-explains rank.** Rebirth in "a low class family" of corpse-workers and scavengers is the fruit of past wrongdoing (MN 129:25.2; MN 135:15.1–15.4), a high wealthy birth the reward of virtue (AN 10.216:8.2). Caste words serve him as insults (AN 5.175:1.1; Snp 3.10:16.3).
- **Kin privilege:** "I give this special privilege to my relatives" (Kd 1:38.11.6–38.11.7).
- **Later layers.** In the Apadāna, birth "in the two high clans" rewards devotion (Tha-ap 503:L25). In the commentaries he agrees the Sakyans wronged a king by giving a slave-woman's daughter instead of "a maiden of equal birth" (DhA 04-03:P61), and reads the rise of the low-born as decline (Ja 77:L24).

**Reading.** Book 11 found the denial that birth confers worth supported, but no call to abolish class. The whole canon adds a harder point: the early texts deny that birth fixes worth yet treat low birth as earned misfortune. Ambedkar drew out a real strand and added a programme the texts lack.

### 9.2 Gender [Strong; mixed]

*Evidence: 291 texts in 31 collections, 120 of them early; 111 lines support, 152 complicate, 96 run against. By group: Nikāyas 93, early Khuddaka 27, Jātaka 80, late Khuddaka 26, Vinaya 39, Abhidhamma 6, Dhammapada stories 20.*

The label is Strong because the trait was recorded often; over two-thirds of the lines complicate or run against it. Section 6.5 covers particular women and the nuns' founding; this covers women as a class.

**Affirming (early texts):**

- "She is able, Ānanda" (AN 8.51:9.6). He would answer "in exactly the same way as the nun Dhammadinnā" (MN 44:30.5).
- Paired discourses treat the sexes identically (AN 8.17–8.18; SN 37.1–37.2).
- He names women's own sufferings, such as leaving their relatives at marriage (SN 37.3:1.3), condemns brahmins who "buy and sell brahmin women" (AN 5.191:3.2), and makes not abducting women a condition of a strong people (DN 16:1.4.17).

**Hostile and restricting (early texts, mostly his own voice):**

- "Ānanda, ladies are irritable, jealous, stingy, and unintelligent" (AN 4.80:2.2–2.5). Women are "an all-round snare of Māra" (AN 5.55:5.3), "filthy, stinking, cowardly, frightening, and treacherous" like a cobra (AN 5.229:2.1–2.3). Many such lines address celibate monks, but they are phrased as general truths.
- No woman can be a Buddha (MN 115:15.1).
- The wife who "endures her husband and does what he says", under threat of the rod, is praised (AN 7.63:11.2–11.3). Sexual misconduct is defined by a woman's guardians (MN 41:8.4); wives are listed among goods (AN 10.74:1.3).

**The Vinaya**, beyond the eight principles (6.5): lustful contact expels a nun but only suspends a monk (Pvr 1.10:7.3–7.4); a husband is "he who possesses her" (Bi-Vb-Pc80:2.1.7–2.1.8); after rapes of nuns travelling alone, travelling alone becomes the nun's offence (Bi-Vb-Ss3:4.14.1).

**Later layers.** In the Jātaka prose, a commentary layer, the Buddha's stock reply to a monk missing his former wife is "this woman does you harm" (Ja 386:L4), often generalised: "Women, Brother, are lustful, profligate, vile, and degraded" (Ja 61:L4). As the Bodhisatta he advises threatening a wife with "the rope or stick" (Ja 130:L13). The Abhidhamma builds the bar on a female Buddha into the Tathāgata's powers (Vb 16:L190).

**Reading.** As in 6.5: spiritual capacity affirmed, institutional subordination enacted, some statements misogynistic by any modern standard. The hostile strand is larger and earlier than v1 implied: it sits in his own voice in the Aṅguttara and grows in the commentaries.

### 9.3 Politics and republics [Moderate]

*Evidence: 91 texts in 17 collections, 30 of them early; 96 lines support, 13 complicate, 1 runs against. By group: Nikāyas 26, early Khuddaka 4, Jātaka 34, late Khuddaka 8, Vinaya 15, Dhammapada stories 4.*

- **Seven conditions of welfare** for the Vajjians, among them frequent assemblies and concord (DN 16; Book 10, passages 1–2). Book 10 noted that the assemblies seated only the ruling clan; "first democracy" overstates the case.
- **The ruler under principle** relies "only on principle" and protects "beasts and birds" (DN 26:5.2). "The whole nation sleeps badly, when the king is unprincipled" (AN 4.70:4.1–4.6).
- **Crime is best reduced** by economic support, not execution (DN 5:11.1–11.13).
- **Saṅgha procedure** runs by open motion, unanimity, and majority vote (Kd 1:28.5.4–28.5.5; Kd 14:9.1.3), but a majority contrary to the Teaching voids the vote (Kd 14:14.26.9–14.26.10).
- **Concord** is a positive value: the ideal person reconciles the divided, "delighting in harmony" (DN 30:2.19.2; Pp 2.4:L210), and a monk who insulted a householder is sent to ask forgiveness (Kd 11:18.5.10–18.5.11).
- **He names no successor** (DN 16:2.25) and **does not challenge monarchy** (Kd 1:40).

### 9.4 Wealth and work [Strong]

*Evidence: 167 texts in 28 collections, 88 of them early; 159 lines support, 30 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 65, early Khuddaka 23, Jātaka 30, late Khuddaka 15, Vinaya 14, Abhidhamma 7, Dhammapada stories 13.*

- **Wealth is good when earned and shared** (AN 4.61; AN 8.54:5.2). He approves a shopkeeper's profit reckoning (AN 3.20:1.6). The practical bent reaches the body: diet for long life, porridge, chew sticks (AN 5.125:2.3; AN 5.207:1.3; AN 5.208:1.3).
- **Five trades are wrong livelihood** (AN 5.177:1.3).
- **Poverty breeds theft** (DN 26:14.1); the poor are jailed for petty theft (AN 3.100:9.1–9.2).
- **Wealth relativised:** "Growth of wealth, mendicants, is a small thing" (AN 1.71–81:1.1); he gave up a "delicate lifestyle" (AN 3.39:1.1–3.4).
- **Complications.** Poverty is the fruit of past stinginess (MN 135:13.1–13.3), as in 9.1. Merit multiplies when supported monks meditate (AN 4.51:1.4–6.2), and critics answer: "I plough and sow, ascetic, and then I eat" (SN 7.11:2.5–2.6).

### 9.5 Violence and war [Moderate; mixed]

*Evidence: 112 texts in 21 collections, 43 of them early; 91 lines support, 42 complicate, 5 run against. By group: Nikāyas 35, early Khuddaka 8, Jātaka 38, late Khuddaka 8, Vinaya 11, Abhidhamma 2, Dhammapada stories 10.*

**Against violence:**

- **Personal non-violence is absolute** (MN 21:20.1; Dhp 3–5, Book 11): "Don’t kill or cause others to kill, don’t conquer or encourage others to conquer" (AN 8.1:7.1–7.2).
- **Causes.** "A killer creates a killer" (SN 3.15:9.1–9.2); war comes from sensual desire (MN 13:11.1). He wonders whether one can rule "without killing or having someone kill for you" (SN 4.20:1.3).
- **State killing judged.** An executioner becomes a tormented ghost (SN 19.16:1.5); a monk who urges "Kill him with a single blow" is expelled (Bu-Vb-Pj3:5.32.2–5.32.3).
- **Soldiers.** One who dies killing with intent is reborn in hell, he says, reluctantly (SN 42.3:2.7–2.11).

**Complicating:**

- **No anti-war programme.** He does not forbid kings war and bars soldiers from ordination at the king's request (Kd 1:40.4.9).
- **State violence as illustration.** Royal tortures serve as neutral examples (AN 2.1–10:2.6; MN 129:4.2). The ideal king's sons go "crushing the armies of my enemies" (AN 7.62:2.6).
- **Later layers.** In the Jātaka prose the Bodhisatta wins kingdoms in battle (Ja 416:L6) and the Buddha praises a monk's battle plan (Ja 492:L6). In the Dhammapada stories victims of a mass burning "suffered precisely that form of death which they deserved" (DhA 10-07:P27).

The label falls from v1's Strong: early attestation is moderate, and almost a third of the lines complicate.

### 9.6 Animals and nature [Moderate]

*Evidence: 94 texts in 22 collections, 31 of them early; 90 lines support, 17 complicate, 1 runs against. By group: Nikāyas 23, early Khuddaka 8, Jātaka 24, late Khuddaka 7, Vinaya 21, Abhidhamma 2, Dhammapada stories 9.*

- **Against animal sacrifice** (DN 5:18.1): "Neither with feet nor with horns do cows harm anyone at all" (Snp 2.7:27.1–27.2).
- **Regard for small and "one-sensed" life.** The rains retreat answered complaints of monks "trampling down the green grass, oppressing one-sensed life" (Kd 3:1.2.2); cutting trees became an offence (Bu-Vb-Pc11:1.18); killing animals became one after a monk shot crows (Bu-Vb-Pc61:1.16.1).
- **Love of forests and clean rivers** (MN 26:17.4; MN 36:16.2).
- **Limits:**
  - Meat "pure in three respects" is allowed (Kd 17:3.15.15–3.15.16; MN 55:5.1–5.2).
  - Animal birth is a punishment (AN 10.216:4.2–4.4); animals cannot ordain (Kd 1:63.4.2).
  - Several "green" rules respond to public opinion.
  - Book 3 found that plants as sentient beings "lacks support in early Buddhism".
