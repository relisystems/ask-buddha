## 7. Teaching method

### 7.1 Questions that use the listener's own premises [Strong]
*Evidence: 146 texts in 11 collections, 103 of them early; 158 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 100, early Khuddaka 3, Jātaka 18, late Khuddaka 3, Vinaya 5, Dhammapada stories 17.*

"What do you think, Kālāmas?" (AN 3.65:5.1–24.2). He leads Lohicca from his own estate to his king's to the teacher's duty (DN 12:10.1–13.9), and Saccaka from a king's power over his realm to his own lack of power over form (MN 35:12.1–13.4). "What can I do about that, worthy Gotama? I am the one who shows the way," the accountant says, and the Buddha applies it to himself (MN 107:14.25–14.32).

### 7.2 Similes chosen for the listener [Moderate]
*Evidence: 77 texts in 15 collections, 30 of them early; 78 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 25, early Khuddaka 5, Jātaka 19, late Khuddaka 10, Vinaya 8, Dhammapada stories 10.*

See section 3.8. The road to Rājagaha for an accountant (MN 107:14.8–14.32), the harp for the former musician Soṇa (AN 6.55:4.2), the Fire Sermon for former fire-worshippers (Kd 1:21.2.2–21.2.6). "I’ve made up this simile to make a point" (MN 19:26.1–26.2).

### 7.3 A graduated path [Moderate]
*Evidence: 95 texts in 24 collections, 52 of them early; 98 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 43, early Khuddaka 9, Jātaka 4, late Khuddaka 12, Vinaya 10, Abhidhamma 9, Dhammapada stories 8.*

"Gradual training, gradual progress" (MN 107:2.12–3.3); awakening comes "not abruptly" (AN 8.19:10.7). "If you practice partially you succeed partially" (SN 48.14:1.6). A Dhammapada story allows arahantship in the raising of a foot (DhA 08-11:P22).

### 7.4 Adapting to the listener [Strong]
*Evidence: 212 texts in 25 collections, 76 of them early; 230 lines support, 3 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 63, early Khuddaka 13, Jātaka 37, late Khuddaka 38, Vinaya 5, Abhidhamma 6, Dhammapada stories 50.*

- He sees beings "with little dust in their eyes, and some with much dust" (MN 26:21.2), weighs Sakka's sincerity (DN 21:1.13.2), spares a confused questioner by silence (SN 44.10:2.8), and declares rebirths to raise morale (DN 18).
- **Timing.** He acts when qualities "have ripened in Rāhula" (MN 147:1.4–1.5); the Dhammapada stories make this ripeness-timing a routine (DhA 02-01:P165; DhA 03-07:P13; DhA 03-08:P13).
- **Delegation.** He leaves brief summaries for Mahākaccāna and Sāriputta to expand, then endorses them (MN 133:12.1; MN 138:22.1; MN 141:5.7).
- **Complications.** He answers a householder in a monk's terms (SN 35.124:2.1–2.2), and in a commentary teaches knowing he will arouse a woman's hatred (DhA 02-01:P182).

### 7.5 Taking over terms and rites [Strong]
*Evidence: 205 texts in 26 collections, 145 of them early; 222 lines support, 3 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 111, early Khuddaka 34, Jātaka 15, late Khuddaka 15, Vinaya 5, Abhidhamma 4, Dhammapada stories 21.*

He reinterprets the six directions (Book 8), sacrifice (DN 5:22.4–22.5), and "brahmin" (Book 11), and lights "only the inner flame" (SN 7.9:13.1–13.2). He calls a king's praise "shrines to the teaching" (MN 89:21.2). Yet Snp 5.4:4.3–4.4 rejects sacrifice outright, and his verses in DN 16 endorse offerings to local deities (DN 16:1.31.6–1.31.9).

### 7.6 Humour and irony [Moderate]
*Evidence: 26 texts in 12 collections, 17 of them early; 28 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 11, early Khuddaka 6, Jātaka 3, late Khuddaka 1, Vinaya 3, Dhammapada stories 2.*

See section 5.4. A rival's perfect ascetic would be "a little baby boy" (MN 78:8.2–8.3); even bandits stay together, he tells quarrelling monks (Kd 10:3.1.27–3.1.31).

### 7.7 Declining to answer [Thin]
*Evidence: 21 texts in 9 collections, 15 of them early; 21 lines support, 2 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 14, early Khuddaka 1, Jātaka 1, late Khuddaka 3, Dhammapada stories 2.*

- "Enough, chief, let it be. Don't ask me that," he says twice to a soldier, before telling him that men who die killing in battle go to hell (SN 42.3:1.5–2.11). Pressed a third time, he usually answers (SN 42.2:2.1–2.4; MN 57:2.12–2.14). "Let it be, Gotami" (Kd 20:1.1.5).
- He keeps the criteria of MN 58: truth, benefit, and timing (MN 58:8.1–8.6). He would not repeat things seen or heard if they make unskilful qualities grow (AN 4.183:4.1).
- **Withholding for effect.** He held one exposition back "Up until now" (AN 9.12:11.3), and kept quiet about a tormented being lest others disbelieve him (SN 19.1:4.4–4.5); a commentary has him speak once "I have Moggallāna for my witness" (DhA 10-06:P14).

### 7.8 Silence [Moderate]
*Evidence: 29 texts in 11 collections, 15 of them early; 30 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 11, early Khuddaka 4, Jātaka 2, late Khuddaka 1, Vinaya 5, Dhammapada stories 6.*

He consents by silence (DN 12:5.5; DN 33:1.2.7), and the Saṅgha approves motions by silence (Kd 13:3.3.11–3.3.16). He also refuses by silence (MN 85:7.8; AN 8.20:2.1). He commends "noble silence" (Book 6, passage 2) and stays silent on the undeclared questions (section 3.5). Ānanda fears his silence may be taken for faltering (AN 10.95:9.2–9.3).

### 7.9 Repetition and lists for memory [Moderate]
*Evidence: 70 texts in 22 collections, 48 of them early; 68 lines support, 2 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 39, early Khuddaka 9, Jātaka 1, late Khuddaka 6, Vinaya 7, Abhidhamma 7, Dhammapada stories 1.*

DN 33; Book 1, analysis 7. Refrains (Kp 5:3.4), paired verses (Dhp 1–20), and recitation "in concert, without disputing" (DN 29:17.1). The Abhidhamma repeats to classify, not to teach (Ya 2.1.1:L19). The Peṭakopadesa's exegetes assume each discourse has "nothing lacking or superfluous" (Pe 4:L12; Pe 3:L27).

### 7.10 Example [Moderate]
*Evidence: 68 texts in 15 collections, 43 of them early; 69 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 38, early Khuddaka 5, Jātaka 6, late Khuddaka 5, Vinaya 8, Dhammapada stories 6.*

"The Realized One does as he says, and says as he does" (DN 29:29.5). Washing the sick monk (Kd 8:26); lying down "mindful and aware" when tired (DN 33:1.5.7); one meal a day, commended from his own health (MN 65:2.1–2.2). Juniors copy seniors' routine without their detachment (SN 20.9:4.6).

### 7.11 No guarantee of results [Moderate]
*Evidence: 86 texts in 21 collections, 50 of them early; 78 lines support, 5 complicate, 10 run against. By group: Nikāyas 38, early Khuddaka 12, Jātaka 10, late Khuddaka 7, Vinaya 3, Abhidhamma 7, Dhammapada stories 9.*

"The Realized One is the one who shows the way" (MN 107:14.32); "Some succeed, while others fail" (MN 107:12.3). Teachers are accountable for what they teach, and pupils for whether they listen (DN 12:16.1–18.15). His first hearer, Upaka, takes a wrong turn (MN 26:25.27). The label hides a counter-strand in his own early words: mindfulness "for seven days" brings awakening or non-return (DN 22:22.22), and "I guarantee you non-return" (Iti 1:2.4–2.5). The promise is to whoever practises, not to every listener.

### 7.12 Arousing urgency (saṃvega) [Moderate]
*Evidence: about 37 texts in about 10 collections, 25 of them early; about 38 lines support, 2 complicate, 0 run against. By group, about: Nikāyas 22, early Khuddaka 3, Jātaka 7, late Khuddaka 2, Vinaya 1, Dhammapada stories 2.*

Grasping the four truths outranks putting out a burning head (SN 56.34:2.1); a Buddha's arising is a rare chance (SN 56.48:2.4); "Don’t regret it later!" (MN 19:27.2). Later layers make shock a deliberate tool (Kd 1:20.17.2–20.17.4; DhA 25-11:P13), and the Jātaka Bodhisatta terrifies hearers with hell (Ja 491:L32). It has a cost: one discourse leaves sixty monks vomiting blood (AN 7.72:18.2–18.3), and King Milinda says a rebuke left Sudinna too frightened to attain the path (Mil 5.3.4:L5).
