## 2. Core values and motivations

### 2.1 Ending suffering is the organising aim [Strong]

*Evidence: 216 texts in 34 collections, 108 of them early; 223 lines support, 2 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 83, early Khuddaka 25, Jātaka 21, late Khuddaka 37, Vinaya 17, Abhidhamma 22, Dhammapada stories 11.*

Everything is ranked by its relevance to the end of suffering.

- He declares the four truths because they are "beneficial and relevant to the fundamentals of the spiritual life" (MN 63:8.1–10.3), and holds back the leaves of the grove (SN 56.31:1.2–1.8).
- "In the past, as today, what I describe is suffering and the cessation of suffering" (MN 22:37.5; SN 22.86:13.2).
- The goal is "unshakable freedom of heart" (MN 30:23.2–23.3), with suffering defeated "in this very life" (MN 63:6.3–6.4). A teaching is tested by whether it ends suffering, not by wonders (DN 24:1.4.14).
- A gift of the teaching beats any material gift (AN 2.141–150).

The Vinaya keeps the aim: his teachings are "for the sake of dispassion, not for the sake of passion" (Bu-Vb-Ss2:1.2.11). The Abhidhamma approves a "good righteous wish" to act (Vb 8:L6): the target is craving, not every desire. Both complications are past-life stories: a fast kept to win Sakka's heaven (Ja 543:L50), and Vessantara's liberation bought at his children's cost (Ja 547:L565).

### 2.2 Compassion, expressed mostly as teaching [Strong]

*Evidence: 303 texts in 32 collections, 129 of them early; 321 lines support, 18 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 90, early Khuddaka 39, Jātaka 49, late Khuddaka 69, Vinaya 19, Abhidhamma 10, Dhammapada stories 27.*

- He teaches "because of my compassion for sentient beings" (MN 26:21.1). A stock close: "Out of sympathy, I’ve done what a teacher should do who wants what’s best for their disciples" (MN 8:17.2; MN 106:15.1).
- To a deity who says the freed should not teach: "it’s unworthy for a wise person not to think of the other with sympathy" (SN 10.2:3.3–3.4).
- **Practical care.** He washes a sick monk: "Whoever would nurse me should nurse one who is sick" and "If you don’t nurse one another, who will?" (Kd 8:26.1.1–26.3.13). Dying, he shields Cunda from blame (DN 16:4.42.3; Book 10).

**Its limits**, sharper across the canon than in v1:

- He first inclines not to teach, finding it "wearying and troublesome for me" (MN 26:19.6; Kd 1:5.4.1; DhA 01-08:P34), and is silent on how much of the world he will save (AN 10.95:8.1–11.1).
- His inspiration depends on the listener's approach (AN 8.82:1.3–1.4), even a good voice (AN 10.83:4.3). Benefiting oneself ranks above benefiting only others (AN 4.95:3.1–3.3; Dhp 157–166).
- He gives visitors a talk "concerned only with sending them away" (MN 122:6.4), and meets a wanderer in agony with only a verse (Ud 2.6:6.2).
- The Abhidhamma ranks love and compassion as mundane states that do not lead to release (Vb 13:L152).

### 2.3 Truthfulness [Strong]

*Evidence: 225 texts in 32 collections, 103 of them early; 223 lines support, 24 complicate, 8 run against. By group: Nikāyas 83, early Khuddaka 20, Jātaka 43, late Khuddaka 19, Vinaya 31, Abhidhamma 7, Dhammapada stories 22.*

- Six rules of speech (MN 58:8.1–8.6); "I will not tell a lie, even for a joke" (MN 61:7.9–7.10).
- He refuses the gods' nectar as "a lie on my part" (MN 36:27.1–27.8), and announced awakening only once his understanding was complete (SN 22.26:2.1).
- No secret doctrine: no "closed fist of a tutor" (DN 16:2.25.2–2.25.3). He insists on accurate report and denies misquotation (MN 71:6.2; MN 136:5.2).
- A good witness, "Not knowing, they say 'I don't know'" (MN 41:13.2); faith is held as faith (MN 95:15.3–15.4). The texts record his failures (MN 26:25.27).

**Where it bends.** The early exception is Nanda: "I guarantee you five hundred dove-footed nymphs" (Ud 3.2:10.1–10.2). The commentaries widen the gap. He tells Kisā Gotamī he knows a cure for her dead child (DhA 08-13:P20), and withholds a queen's hell rebirth lest the king stop his offerings (DhA 11-06:P19). The Vinaya has him hide Yasa from his searching father by psychic power (Kd 1:7.8.2). As the Bodhisatta he lies outright (Ja 201:L9; Ja 80:L9), deceives Maddī ("With this deceit he consoled her", Ja 547:L522), and feigns deafness for sixteen years, praised as a perfection (Cp 26:10.1–10.4).

### 2.4 Non-harm [Strong]

*Evidence: 267 texts in 32 collections, 104 of them early; 244 lines support, 42 complicate, 15 run against. By group: Nikāyas 72, early Khuddaka 32, Jātaka 81, late Khuddaka 27, Vinaya 23, Abhidhamma 10, Dhammapada stories 22.*

- "Renouncing the rod and the sword" (MN 41:12.2); the golden rule (SN 55.7:4.3–4.7); "Creatures love happiness" (Ud 2.3:1.3–3.4); the saw simile (MN 21:20.1).
- Guilt extends to encouraging and approving killing (AN 3.163–182:1.3). He will not praise violent sacrifice (AN 4.39:1.6–1.7; DN 5:18.1–18.5). The Vinaya bans silk blankets once he hears how silk is made (Bu-Vb-NP11:1.23.1).

**Hard cases.**

- **Early.** Twice he warns that whoever will not answer a question "asked three times by the Buddha" will have their head explode (DN 3:1.20.12; MN 35:13.12–13.13). He declares Channa's suicide blameless (MN 144:13.11).
- **Vinaya.** Meat is allowed unless killed for the monk (Kd 6:31.14.4–31.14.6). A monk whose assault killed a girl gets only a suspension-level offence under the rule on sexual intercourse, which is the rule the case is filed under (Bu-Vb-Pj1:10.4.1–10.4.5).
- **Jātaka prose, a commentary layer**, which holds most of the 57 hard lines. The Bodhisatta kills (Ja 128:L10), poisons dice (Ja 91:L6), advises beating a queen (Ja 386:L17), and orders an ingrate speared (Ja 445:L32). The frame-story Buddha praises a servant girl who killed a robber (Ja 419:L4). The narrator admits Bodhisattas are sometimes "born as wicked men" (Ja 279:L13).

### 2.5 Peace and happiness as positive goods [Strong]

*Evidence: 185 texts in 28 collections, 117 of them early; 194 lines support, 5 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 93, early Khuddaka 24, Jātaka 9, late Khuddaka 16, Vinaya 13, Abhidhamma 18, Dhammapada stories 12.*

- "Why am I afraid of that pleasure...?" (MN 36:32.2). The pleasure of awakening "should not be feared, I say" (MN 139:9.18–9.19).
- The purified life is not grim: "Such a life is blissful" (DN 9:40.5–40.6). "I see happiness for myself in this life" (MN 4:34.5). Seven days of "absolute happiness" (MN 14:21.13–21.19).
- Pasenadi sees monks "always smiling and joyful" (MN 89:12.10). The Vinaya calls removing the conceit ‘I am’ "the highest bliss" (Kd 1:3.4.1–3.4.8).

Happiness is valued selectively: some sadness should be cultivated (DN 21:2.3.5–2.3.6), and the Paṭṭhāna treats rapture as a neutral tone that can accompany theft (Paṭṭhāna 1.8:L143).

### 2.6 What he does not seek, and what he guards [Strong]

*Evidence: 303 texts in 31 collections, 125 of them early; 307 lines support, 33 complicate, 7 run against. By group: Nikāyas 103, early Khuddaka 22, Jātaka 65, late Khuddaka 36, Vinaya 44, Abhidhamma 7, Dhammapada stories 26.*

- **Not fame, followers, or veneration.** Fame tests a teacher (MN 47:8.3–8.7); no successor (DN 16:2.25.4–2.25.6); "Let your tutor remain your tutor" (DN 25:23.2–23.4). "One who sees the teaching sees me" (SN 22.87:3.1–3.2). Gain and honour "are grim, bitter, and harsh" (SN 17.1:2.1).
- **But he guards the community.** Vinaya rulings cite public confidence (Bi-Vb-Pc95:1.9; Bu-Vb-Sk37:1.14). He endorses a joint recitation "so that this spiritual path may last for a long time" (DN 33:1.6.2–1.7.6).

**Complications.** "I don’t see even a single one who has reached the peak of material things and fame like me" (DN 29:16.1). The merit scales put gifts to himself and his Saṅgha at the top (MN 142:6.1; AN 9.20:8.4). In the Apadāna he promises sixty thousand women for a seat (Tha-ap 409:L32). The Jātaka Bodhisatta is ruled by lust and pride (Ja 527:L20; Ja 547:L5).

Whether the institutional concern is the founder's or the redactors' cannot be settled.

### 2.7 Gratitude, filial duty, and loyal friendship [Moderate]

*Evidence: about 51 texts in about 9 collections, 13 of them early; 60 lines support, 2 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 12, early Khuddaka 1, Jātaka 30, late Khuddaka 4, Vinaya 2, Dhammapada stories 2.*

- He chooses his first hearers because "The group of five mendicants were very helpful to me" (MN 26:24.5; Kd 1:6.5.5).
- "We won’t forget even a small thing done for us" (SN 20.12:1.6–1.7). A grateful person is as rare as a Buddha (AN 3.114:1.3); a teacher is not easily repaid (AN 3.24:4.2).
- Parents cannot be repaid by carrying them for a lifetime, only by establishing them in faith and virtue (AN 2.32–41:1.4, 1.9). Neglecting them brings downfall (Snp 1.6:9.1–9.3).
- The true friend does not desert you "in times of trouble" (AN 7.36:1.3).
- The Vinaya: "superior people have gratitude" (Kd 1:28.2.2–28.3.1). In the Jātaka prose, a commentary layer, filial piety is a leading theme: the Bodhisatta feeds blind parents (Ja 501:L5), and the frame-story Buddha praises monks who support their mothers (Ja 540:L4).

Renunciation pulls the other way: parents lament that Gotama took their only son (Ja 14:L6), and a monk's affectionate touch of his mother is an offence (Bu-Vb-Ss2:4.1.6–4.1.7). See also 6.6.
