## 3. Cognitive style

### 3.1 Analysis and lists [Strong]

*Evidence: 139 texts in 27 collections, 70 of them early; 142 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 62, early Khuddaka 8, Jātaka 3, late Khuddaka 13, Vinaya 19, Abhidhamma 29, Dhammapada stories 5.*

- Skill in elements, sense fields, and aggregates marks an "astute inquirer" (MN 115:2.12–3.3). "I’ve taught the Dhamma analytically" (SN 22.81:4.6).
- A question "should have been answered after analyzing it" (MN 136:5.3). He sorts all speculation into sixty-two grounds (DN 1:3.29.1) and each aggregate along eleven dimensions (SN 18.21:1.4).
- Numbered lists, memory technology for an oral community, fill whole collections (AN; DN 33; Book 1, analysis 7) and the Vinaya (Bu-Vb-Pc83:1.3.1). Five titles for one discourse show play within the system (MN 115:20.4). No line complicates the trait.

### 3.2 Causal and conditional reasoning [Strong]

*Evidence: 312 texts in 26 collections, 188 of them early; 331 lines support, 7 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 162, early Khuddaka 26, Jātaka 30, late Khuddaka 25, Vinaya 8, Abhidhamma 44, Dhammapada stories 17.*

- **The core form.** "When this exists, this comes to be" (MN 79:7.7; SN 12.15:3.4–3.9), a regularity holding "whether Realized Ones arise or not" (SN 12.20:2.3). The Vinaya makes it his first reflection after awakening (Kd 1:1.2.2).
- **Backward search.** Vipassī works back link by link "through rational application of mind" (DN 14:2.18.5–2.18.7).
- **The mind and society.** Perceptions "arise and cease with cause and reason" (DN 9:7.4–7.5); jealousy and stinginess come from "the liked and the disliked" (DN 21:2.2.5–2.2.11); he traces the Jains' pain to present exertion, not past deeds (MN 101:15.3). Desire drives crime (MN 13:14.1–14.2); poverty breeds killing (DN 26:10.4–10.5).
- **Change is possible.** "This mind, mendicants, is radiant. But it is corrupted by visiting corruptions." (AN 1.51–60:1.1–1.2). "It is possible to give up the unskillful. If it wasn’t possible, I wouldn’t say" so (AN 2.11–20:1.1–1.3).

**Complications.** The causal form carries cosmological content: earthquakes from winds beside seven psychic or Buddha-related causes (DN 16:3.13.3); drought from human immorality (AN 3.56:3.2–3.3). Dhp 116–128 says "nothing bad happens unless you do bad", which SN 36.21 denies.

### 3.3 The middle way [Strong]

*Evidence: 122 texts in 24 collections, 71 of them early; 131 lines support, 4 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 54, early Khuddaka 17, Jātaka 8, late Khuddaka 18, Vinaya 6, Abhidhamma 16, Dhammapada stories 3.*

- **Between doctrines.** "All exists" and "all does not exist" (SN 12.15:3.1–3.3); soul and body (SN 12.35:1.8–1.10).
- **Between ways of life.** Austerity abandoned, indulgence rejected (MN 36:30.1–33.7; Book 1); striving that does not "forsake legitimate pleasure" (MN 101:23.2).
- **As a craft.** The lute (Kd 5:1.15.11–1.16.3); the quail gripped too tightly (MN 128:22.5); the flood crossed "neither standing nor swimming" (SN 1.1:1.7–1.9).
- **In conduct and institutions.** Neither serve everyone nor no one (MN 96:5.1); balanced finances (AN 8.54:5.2–5.6); austerities optional (Kd 17:3.15.10–3.15.14).

**Complications.** As a last resort he allows crushing "mind with mind" (MN 20:7.2). The sage is "not stuck in the middle" either (Snp 5.3:3.1–3.3).

### 3.4 Pragmatism and tests by outcome [Strong]

*Evidence: 180 texts in 23 collections, 103 of them early; 200 lines support, 4 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 92, early Khuddaka 11, Jātaka 26, late Khuddaka 9, Vinaya 25, Abhidhamma 4, Dhammapada stories 13.*

- **Cultivate by results** (DN 21:2.3.9–2.3.15); check every act before, during, and after (MN 61:8.2–10.2).
- **The teaching is a tool**: a raft "for crossing over, not for holding on" (MN 22:13.1–14.1); the poisoned arrow (MN 63:5.6–5.35).
- **Checkable outcomes.** Not eating at night leaves monks "healthy and well" (MN 70:2.3–2.4); drink has six visible harms (DN 31:8.2; Bu-Vb-Pc51:1.40); the ethical life is "a sure bet" either way (SN 42.13:23.5–23.6); "if you practice partially you succeed partially" (SN 48.14:1.6).

**Complications.** He justifies declaring rebirths by their inspiring effect (MN 68:9.4–9.5). The Vinaya tells candidates the hardships only after ordination (Kd 1:31.1.8–31.1.10), and allows dung, urine, ash, and clay for snakebite (Kd 6:14.6.3–14.6.4).

### 3.5 Silence on questions he will not answer [Moderate]

*Evidence: 62 texts in 16 collections, 47 of them early; 58 lines support, 8 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 42, early Khuddaka 5, late Khuddaka 9, Vinaya 1, Abhidhamma 5.*

The label falls from v1's Strong because the trait is narrow (the same ten questions recur in a few clusters), not because the evidence is weak.

- **Undeclared views** (MN 63:2.2, 8.1–8.3), though he knows and sees (DN 6:16.12–16.14). Setting a question aside is one of four ways to answer (AN 4.42:1.6).
- **Two reasons in one case** (SN 44.10:1.4–2.8); **the extinguished fire** (MN 72:19.2–20.3); **four unthinkables** (AN 4.77:1.1–1.11).
- **The goal by negation.** "There is, mendicants, that dimension where there is no earth, no water, no fire, no wind" (Ud 8.1:3.1; Ud 8.3:3.1; Ud 1.10:14.1–14.3). For the freed, "eradicated, too, are all ways of speech" (Snp 5.7:8.4–8.5).
- The nuns Dhammadinnā and Khemā hold the same line (MN 44:29.14; SN 44.1:4.3).

**Complications.** He condemns evasive teachers (DN 1:2.24.7), and a critic calls him one "who refrains from making statements" (AN 10.94:5.2). Milinda reads the silence as ignorance or concealment (Mil 5.2.2:1.3).

### 3.6 Reframing the question [Strong]

*Evidence: 125 texts in 20 collections, 88 of them early; 129 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 77, early Khuddaka 11, Jātaka 8, late Khuddaka 7, Vinaya 3, Abhidhamma 3, Dhammapada stories 16.*

- "This is not how the question should be asked" (DN 11:85.8–85.10). "That’s not a cogent question… I don’t speak of one who consumes" (SN 12.12:2.3–2.4).
- Offered two options, he takes neither: "Neither. Rather, the desire and greed for them is the grasping there" (SN 22.82:4.4). "This matter is not categorical" (MN 58:6.3). He redefines delusion (MN 36:46.3) and will "not be" a god, spirit, or human, but a Buddha (AN 4.36:2.1–3.1).
- He relocates "the world" into "this fathom-long carcass" (SN 2.26:5.2), and rewrites deities' verses, turning merit-making into renunciation (SN 1.3:3.4).
- In the Vinaya he asks young men chasing a woman whether they should not "search for yourselves" (Kd 1:14.3.1–14.3.2).

### 3.7 Convention and analysis [Moderate]

*Evidence: 52 texts in 18 collections, 28 of them early; 56 lines support, 0 complicate, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 23, early Khuddaka 5, Jātaka 2, late Khuddaka 6, Vinaya 4, Abhidhamma 12.*

- He uses "the world’s common usages… without getting stuck on them" (DN 9:53.5). Names and clans are "mere convention" (MN 98:12.1–12.4); monks should accept local words for a bowl (MN 139:12.9–12.10).
- **Several valid framings.** Two disputants who counted feelings differently were both "quite correct" (SN 36.19:5.1–5.2), for he has counted them as two, three, five, or more (SN 36.20:1.1). Monks who answered differently each spoke from their own focus, like people who saw a tree in different seasons (SN 35.245:7.16). These are framings within his teaching, not relativism about rival teachings (12.9).
- **Later layers.** The Vinaya defines low status as whatever a country despises (Bu-Vb-Pc2:2.1.30). Nāgasena finds no person "in the absolute sense" (Mil 3.1.1:7.2); the Kathāvatthu says the eye does not "see" (Kv 18.9).

### 3.8 Concrete imagination [Strong]

*Evidence: 448 texts in 29 collections, 321 of them early; 472 lines support, 1 complicates, 0 run against. By group: Nikāyas 282, early Khuddaka 39, Jātaka 34, late Khuddaka 29, Vinaya 15, Abhidhamma 15, Dhammapada stories 34.*

The best-attested trait in this section. His similes come from work and nature:

- a horse trainer (MN 107:2.9–3.3), a lathe-turner (DN 22:2.8)
- a queue of blind men (DN 13:15.2), a cobra grasped by the tail (MN 22:10.11–10.13)
- elephant calves eating muddy lotus bulbs (SN 20.9:3.1–4.13), a ship's land-spotting bird (DN 11:85.2–85.7)
- an old cart held with straps (DN 16:2.25.11); in the Vinaya, flowers unstrung on a plank (Bu-Vb-Pj1:3.2.6)

He teaches by demonstration too, with the dregs in a washing pot (MN 61:3.1–3.4). His log similes were "neither supernaturally inspired, nor learned before in the past" (MN 36:17.1). Some images carry their time: unwilling pupils are like a woman pressed by unwanted advances (DN 12:16.12). The one complication is his own: "it’s not easy to give a simile for how quickly the mind changes" (AN 1.41–50:1.2).

### 3.9 The Abhidhamma: the community's extension, not his voice [Strong in the Abhidhamma]

*Evidence: 154 texts in 18 collections, 3 of them early; 167 lines support, 6 complicate, 1 runs against. By group: Nikāyas 3, late Khuddaka 27, Vinaya 9, Abhidhamma 112, Dhammapada stories 3.*

This label describes the Abhidhamma itself, not a trait of the Buddha: the scholastic layers systematise in his name while he does not speak.

- **Roots.** Sāriputta's numbered recitations in the Nikāyas, with the Buddha silent (DN 33:1.7.10; DN 34:1.1.7–1.1.10).
- **Method marked as an addition.** The Vibhaṅga separates "Analysis According to the Discourses" from "Analysis According to Abhidhamma" (Vb 1:L66–L67), and has links condition each other both ways (Vb 6:L50).
- **Exhaustive permutation and consistency testing** (Ya 3.2.3; Kv 11.6), with sutta quotation as the final court (Kv 7.3 cites DN 11).
- **The same habit elsewhere.** The Paṭisambhidāmagga turns three liberations into sixty-eight (Ps 1.5:L5); the Netti credits its method to Mahākaccāna and grades the Buddha's own answers (Ne 1:L6; Ne 16:L10); the Parivāra names its later compiler (Pvr 21:81.1–82.3).

**Against.** Later tradition claims the Abhidhamma as his voice, preached to the gods (Pvr 3:5.2–5.4; Mil 6.4.2:L10; DhA 14-02:P187; Bv 25:5.1–6.4). No text shows him speaking it.

Use these texts only for cognitive style: the tradition remembered a mind systematic to the point of completeness. The Kathāvatthu settles disputes by quotation, a more authority-based method than AN 3.65.
