## 1. Purpose and limits

### What this profile is

This is a literary and psychological portrait of the Buddha as the Pali Tipiṭaka depicts him: what he says, how he reasons, how he treats people, and what he values. It is the working reference for writing "Buddha's View from a Modern Perspective" sections in the Thripitaka Analysis Series. Version 2 rebuilds the approved first version (22 November 2026) on a reading of the whole canon.

### What it is not

- **It is not a claim about the historical person's mind.** The texts were composed, recited, edited, and transmitted by a community over several centuries, first orally and then in writing. Different layers come from different periods. The Vinaya's legal matrices, the Abhidhamma, the Buddhavaṃsa, the Apadāna, and the Jātaka prose frames are later than most of the discourses, and the Dhammapada stories are commentary.
- **The portrait is the tradition's.** When this profile says "the Buddha is patient", it means "the texts portray him as patient". Every trait is a feature of a literary figure built by many hands. That figure may preserve real memories of a teacher, but this profile cannot separate memory from idealisation, and does not try.
- **Modern personality terms are interpretive.** Big Five estimates and similar labels (section 5.8) apply a modern vocabulary to a composite literary figure. They are aids to writing, not findings.

### Evidence base

1. **The whole canon, read once.** Every text of the Pali Tipiṭaka on SuttaCentral (7,288 texts, about 2.8 million Pali words), with the English of Bhikkhu Sujato and Bhikkhu Brahmali where it exists and older translations elsewhere, plus the Dhammapada commentary's stories (308 pages, in Burlingame's translation as revised by Ānandajoti). Repeated stock passages were removed by script (8.8 per cent of the English) so that each was read once.
2. **Extraction.** Reading agents, each with the same brief, read the canon in 108 chunks of about 100,000 tokens, and one more read a sample of the 869 texts with no English (all of the Buddhavaṃsa; samples of the Niddesas, Vimānavatthu, Petavatthu, and Paṭṭhāna). For each text they recorded the clearest evidence for each trait of the first version (at most three lines per trait per text), with the speaker, the layer, and whether the line supports the trait, complicates it, or runs against it. They also flagged, for every text, whether the Buddha speaks, whether gods, rebirth, or wonders appear, and proposed traits the first version lacked.
3. **Checks.** A script compared every quotation with the cited segments: 99.8 per cent matched word for word. The 23 lines that did not are excluded from this profile.
4. **The eleven books of the series** (Books 1 to 11), whose translations and verdicts the first version drew on.

In all: **13,820 evidence lines from 4,076 texts**, out of 6,781 texts read and flagged. 12,337 lines support a trait, 1,266 complicate it, and 217 run against it. 8,466 lines quote the Buddha himself; the rest are narration (2,772), disciples (1,322), other people (959), and deities (301).

### Attestation labels

The labels now follow counts. "Texts" means distinct texts with evidence for the trait; "early" means the four main Nikāyas and the early Khuddaka texts, without the Jātaka prose. The seven groups are the four Nikāyas, the early Khuddaka, the Jātaka, the late Khuddaka, the Vinaya, the Abhidhamma, and the Dhammapada stories.

- **[Strong]:** at least 100 texts, in at least 3 of the 7 groups, at least 50 of them early.
- **[Moderate]:** at least 25 texts, at least 10 of them early.
- **[Thin]:** anything less.
- **"Strong in the Vinaya"** (or another group) is added when a trait falls short overall but has at least 50 texts in one later group; "Moderate in the ..." at 25.
- **"mixed"** is added when a quarter or more of a trait's lines complicate it or run against it.

The rules were set after looking at the tallies; the first version's looser rule would have called 64 of 70 traits Strong. Each trait opens with an evidence line giving its counts. The counts measure how often the reading agents recorded a trait, not how true it is: the agents searched for the traits of the first version, so traits it lacked are undercounted (the new subsections give approximate counts), and short formulaic texts count as much as long ones.

### A warning from the whole canon

The famous "rational, psychological Buddha" rests on a selection. The first version's random sample found gods, rebirth, or past lives in 17 of 30 suttas (57 per cent). The whole canon puts the share lower, at 44 per cent of all early texts and 42 per cent of the four Nikāyas, but confirms the picture.

| Group | Texts | Buddha speaks | Gods | Rebirth or past lives | Wonders |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Four main Nikāyas | 3,413 | 92% | 25% | 29% | 9% |
| Other early Khuddaka | 691 | 42% | 31% | 46% | 15% |
| Vinaya | 422 | 87% | 12% | 9% | 8% |
| Late Khuddaka | 998 | 33% | 44% | 78% | 28% |
| Abhidhamma | 402 | 19% | 20% | 40% | 15% |
| Jātaka (prose: commentary) | 547 | 97% | 58% | 97% | 41% |
| Dhammapada stories (commentary) | 308 | 99% | 45% | 60% | 46% |

- In the four main Nikāyas, 42 per cent of texts mention gods, rebirth, or wonders. The share rises with length: 33 of the 34 long discourses (DN) and 119 of the 152 middle-length (MN), against 43 per cent of the short connected discourses (SN) and 35 per cent of the numbered ones (AN), many of which are lists.
- The supernatural grows layer by layer: modest in the Vinaya, larger in the early Khuddaka, dominant in the late Khuddaka and the commentaries.
- The Buddha is largely absent from the Abhidhamma; where he appears, it is mostly through quoted suttas (section 3.9).

Any modern reading must start from the whole figure, not the most quotable parts.

### Conventions

- Citations use SuttaCentral uids and segment numbers: `MN 36:27.7` means Majjhima Nikāya 36, segment 27.7. Older translations number paragraphs: `Ja 544:L4` is paragraph 4 of Jātaka 544.
- Vinaya: `Kd 17:3.1.12` = `pli-tv-kd17:3.1.12` (Khandhaka); `Bu-Vb` = monks' Vibhaṅga; `Bi-Vb` = nuns' Vibhaṅga (for example `Bu-Vb-Sk37` = `pli-tv-bu-vb-sk37`); `Pvr` = Parivāra.
- The Dhammapada stories: `DhA 01-09:P3` is chapter 1, story 9, paragraph 3.
- English follows Bhikkhu Sujato (suttas) and Bhikkhu Brahmali (Vinaya), and the older translations where theirs is lacking (for the Jātaka prose, the Cowell edition). Quotations keep the translator's wording and spelling.
- "Book 7" and so on refer to the series (Book 1 SN 56.11; 2 Snp 2.4; 3 Snp 1.8; 4 Snp 2.1; 5 MN 10; 6 MN 118; 7 AN 3.65; 8 DN 31; 9 SN 22.59; 10 DN 16; 11 Dhammapada).
- The skill's `scripts/lookup.py` prints the Pali and English of any cited segment, from a local copy of the canon when one is installed, otherwise from the SuttaCentral API.
