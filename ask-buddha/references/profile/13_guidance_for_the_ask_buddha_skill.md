## 13. Guidance for the ask-buddha skill

### 13.1 Rules for "Buddha's View from a Modern Perspective"

1. **Third person, always.** Write "the Buddha of the discourses", "the texts portray him as", "on the reading the texts allow". Never voice him in the first person, never write "the Buddha would say" without marking it as inference, and never put modern words in his mouth.
2. **Text first, then interpretation, in separate sentences.** Every section has two layers. First, what the texts say, with citations (uid and segment where possible). Second, how that bears on the modern question, marked as inference: "This suggests", "A reader may infer", "On the most favourable reading".
3. **Be as favourable as the evidence honestly allows, and no more.** Use the favourable readings in section 12. Always state the matching limit, briefly, in the same section. A favourable case that ignores a known counter-text (for example Kd 20 on nuns, Kd 1:47 on slaves, Kd 17:3.15 on meat) is not honest.
4. **Never invent texts, quotations, or segment numbers.** If unsure of a segment, cite the sutta alone or check it with `scripts/lookup.py` (a local copy of the canon, or SuttaCentral). Paraphrase rather than quote when wording is uncertain. Quote translators exactly and name them when quoting.
5. **Never misstate studies.** Cite only studies already cited and checked in the series, or ones you have verified, with author and year. Report effect sizes and caveats as the series does (modest, partial, contested). Do not cite "studies show" without a source.
6. **Keep the layers of the tradition distinct.** Label commentary as commentary. This includes the Jātaka frames, the Dhammapada stories, and Kisāgotamī's mustard seed. Label the Abhidhamma as systematic thought, not his voice. Note late layers (the Buddhavaṃsa, the Apadāna, the Vimānavatthu and Petavatthu with their merit economy, the Vinaya matrices) and parallels that disagree.
7. **Use the attestation labels.** They follow counts (section 1). Build arguments on [Strong] traits, and where it helps, give the count ("in over 300 texts"). Name the group for traits strong in only one ("in the Vinaya"). Mark [Thin] ones ("in one episode", "one text records"). For a trait marked "mixed", state the complication in the same breath.
8. **Do not contradict the Vīmaṃsā section.** The Saddhā and modern-view sections make the best honest case; the Vīmaṃsā tests it. If the case against shows a claim is contradicted (rebirth, cosmology, guaranteed outcomes), the modern view may reframe or set it aside, but must not deny the finding.
9. **No exceptionalism.** Do not call Buddhism "a science", say the Buddha "anticipated" a modern discovery, or say neuroscience "confirms" Nibbāna (Book 1, analysis 6; Book 4). Use "resembles", "converges with", "is consistent with".
10. **Respect his own silences.** Where the texts decline to answer (the undeclared questions, the four unthinkables), the modern view should say so rather than fill the gap.
11. **Tie each modern judgement to a trait.** For example: "Because the texts portray him as testing practices by their results (DN 21:2.4; MN 61), he would most plausibly judge X by Y."
12. **Mark the hard cases when relevant.** Gender, slavery, caste, meat, grief, harsh rebukes, and accommodation to power. One clear sentence is enough. Silence about them reads as advocacy.
13. **Avoid misattributions.** None of the following is in the canon in the form usually quoted:
    - "Believe nothing... unless it agrees with your own reason" (Book 7)
    - "Be a lamp unto yourself" without "with the teaching as your island" (Book 10)
    - "Pain is inevitable, suffering is optional" (modern)
    - "Holding on to anger is like drinking poison" (modern)
    - "You yourself, as much as anybody in the entire universe, deserve your love" (modern; the nearest canonical idea is Ud 5.1:6.2–6.4, "one finds no one dearer than oneself … so one who desires self-knowledge would harm no other")
14. **British spelling, plain English, sentence-case headings**, matching the series.

### 13.2 A template for each modern-view section

For a book of the series, use the five headings of the skill's SKILL.md instead ("The Buddha's point" to "Why it matters today"); they take precedence. This template is for other questions.

1. **The question today** (one or two sentences).
2. **What the texts show:** two to four cited points, including at least one from outside the passage under analysis.
3. **The trait at work:** name the trait from this profile (for example "pragmatic outcome test, [Strong]").
4. **The favourable modern reading:** clearly marked as inference.
5. **Where the reading stops:** the limit, with its counter-text.
